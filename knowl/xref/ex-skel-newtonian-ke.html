Example 2.3.16. Newtonian kinetic energy as an approximation to relativistic kinetic energy when \(v \ll c\).

Show that the kinetic energy of an object moving with velocity \(v\) can be approximated by the formula \(K \approx \frac{1}{2} m_0v^2\) when \(v \ll c\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution. (Substitute (2.3.7) into (2.3.8) and set \(x = \frac{v^2}{c^2}\text{,}\) so that everything runs through the Taylor series
\begin{equation} \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x}} = 1 + \frac{1}{2}x + \frac{3}{8}x^2 + \frac{5}{16}x^3 + \cdots .\tag{2.3.9} \end{equation}
Substitute back and say which term survives when \(v \ll c\text{.}\))
🔗
When \(v \ll c\text{,}\) the higher order terms in the series become negligible, and we can approximate the kinetic energy as:
\begin{equation} K \approx \frac{1}{2} m_0v^2.\tag{2.3.10} \end{equation}
🔗
Hint.
We can use the Taylor series for \(f(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x}}\) at \(x=0\) to approximate \(K\text{.}\) Use \(x = \frac{v^2}{c^2}\text{.}\)
